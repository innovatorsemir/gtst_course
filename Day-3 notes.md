# Day 3 – Introduction to Cyber Security

## What is Cyber Security?

**Cyber Security** is the practice of protecting **systems, networks, and programs** from digital attacks.

These digital attacks are commonly called **cyberattacks**, and the act of carrying them out is often referred to as **hacking**.

---

## What is Hacking?

**Hacking** refers to exploiting vulnerabilities and bypassing security controls to gain **unauthorized access** to a system, network, or application.

### Ethical Hacking

**Ethical hacking** is the authorized process of finding weaknesses or vulnerabilities in a computer system, web application, or network and reporting them to the organization so they can be fixed.

### Hacking Process

- Search / Find Vulnerabilities
- Exploit Vulnerabilities

> **Meaning:** Find vulnerabilities in a system or web application and exploit them for personal gain (unauthorized hacking) or responsibly report them (ethical hacking).

The person who performs hacking is called a **Hacker**.

---

# Types of Hackers

## Based on Ethics

### Black Hat Hacker
A malicious hacker who illegally exploits vulnerabilities for personal gain or to cause damage.

### White Hat Hacker
An authorized security professional who identifies and fixes vulnerabilities.

### Gray Hat Hacker
An individual who sometimes acts ethically and other times engages in questionable or illegal activities.

---

## Based on Skill Level

### 1. Newbie (Noob)
- Has little or no knowledge of hacking.

### 2. Script Kiddie
A relatively unskilled individual who uses tools, scripts, or programs developed by others, usually for malicious purposes.

### 3. Hacker
- Skilled in hacking.
- Has moderate experience.

### 4. Elite (Pro)
- Highly skilled.
- Extensive experience in cybersecurity and hacking.

---

# Why Do We Learn Cyber Security?

Cyber Security is learned to:

- Protect systems and networks
- Prevent cyberattacks
- Secure sensitive information
- Detect vulnerabilities
- Improve overall security

---

# Who Can Become an Ethical Hacker?

An ethical hacker should have:

- Knowledge of technology and security
- Ability and willingness to learn continuously
- Strong work ethics
- Interest in forensics and cybersecurity

---

# Why Do Hacks Happen?

A cyberattack happens when three elements come together:

**Attack = Motive + Method + Vulnerability**

## Common Motives

- Information theft
- Data manipulation
- Financial gain
- Revenge
- Ransom
- Damaging reputation

---

# Elements of Information Security

## CIA Triad

The foundation of information security consists of three principles:

### 1. Confidentiality
Ensures that information remains private and accessible only to authorized users.

### 2. Integrity
Ensures that data remains accurate, trustworthy, and protected from unauthorized modification.

### 3. Availability
Ensures that information and resources (hardware, software, networks, and data) are available whenever needed.

# Phases of Hacking

## 1. Reconnaissance (Information Gathering)

Collecting information about the target using passive and active methods.

Examples:
- OSINT (Open Source Intelligence)
- Scanning
- Social media research

---

## 2. Scanning

Identifying:

- Open ports
- Running services
- Vulnerabilities

---

## 3. Gaining Access

Exploiting vulnerabilities to gain access to the target system.

---

## 4. Maintaining Access

Keeping access by establishing persistence (such as backdoors).

---

## 5. Clearing Tracks (Clearing Logs)

Removing logs and hiding traces to avoid detection.

---

# Penetration Testing (Pentesting)

Penetration Testing is the process of evaluating the security of a system or network by **simulating a real-world cyberattack**.

Its purpose is to:

- Find vulnerabilities
- Evaluate security controls
- Prepare documentation and security reports

---

# Why Do We Need Penetration Testing?

- Identify threats
- Evaluate security protections and controls
- Assess an organization's security posture
- Evaluate network security
- Improve and upgrade infrastructure

---

# Types of Penetration Testing

## Black Box Testing

- No prior knowledge of the system
- Tester behaves like an external attacker

---

## White Box Testing

- Full knowledge of the system
- Tester behaves like a developer or internal security engineer

---

## Gray Box Testing

- Limited knowledge of the system
- Tester behaves like a normal authenticated user
